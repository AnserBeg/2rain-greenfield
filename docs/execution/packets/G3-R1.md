# G3-R1 — reconciliation the ledger cannot argue with

Lane: BUILD · Base: `1f7b4948` · Tier: Critical.

Closes the three gaps the `g3-completion` ruling left on plan §11.6 build item 11
after reading `G3-P5`'s code: the source-document arm, sweep coverage, and
operator visibility. It adds no scheduler — durable job machinery stays deferred
to G4 — so what lands is an **invocable** reconciliation over a named scope.

## What already existed and was not rebuilt

`G3-P5` verifies an anchor against an expected digest on every aggregate read
(`module-runtime-interpreter.ts:1277-1280`) and, on mismatch, recomputes from the
ledger, persists a discrepancy row, emits an `anchor-discrepancy` observation,
returns the recomputed value, and deliberately preserves the bad anchor
(`:1315-1319`, with `ON DELETE RESTRICT` on the discrepancy foreign key making a
silent repair structurally impossible). None of that is touched or weakened.

## What this packet adds

`packages/postgres-provider/src/inventory-reconciliation-service.ts` —
`PostgresInventoryReconciliationService.reconcile(context, scope)`, plus
`renderInventoryReconciliationReport`.

### R1-a — the source-document arm

For every **posted** `inventory_transaction` line in scope, the reconciler
derives what the ledger must contain from the persisted line and its transaction
type, and compares it against the movements actually linked to that line:

| Transaction type | Declared effect | Movements |
|---|---|---|
| `adjustment`, `count_correction` | the line's signed quantity at its single declared location | 1 |
| `transfer` | `-quantity` at `from_location`, `+quantity` at `to_location` | 2 |

Findings name the transaction, the line, the location, and both quantities:
`SOURCE_DOCUMENT_QUANTITY_DIVERGED`, `SOURCE_DOCUMENT_MOVEMENT_COUNT_DIVERGED`,
`SOURCE_DOCUMENT_ITEM_DIVERGED`, `SOURCE_DOCUMENT_UNIT_DIVERGED`,
`SOURCE_DOCUMENT_SOURCE_IDENTITY_DIVERGED`,
`SOURCE_DOCUMENT_EFFECTIVE_AT_DIVERGED`, and — for the mirror case — every
movement with no posted source document at all,
`SOURCE_DOCUMENT_MISSING_FOR_MOVEMENT`.

### The comparison set, enumerated — and NOT complete

Three combined-tree review rounds each found "one more field nobody compares".
The set below is what the arm compares today. **It was written as a complete
enumeration and the next review round immediately found two more fields missing
from it** (`reason_code` / `reason_narrative`, which correct posting does copy
from the header to every movement, and the movement's own direct transaction
foreign key, which can name a different transaction than its line's). Those are
open, recorded below, and deliberately not fixed — see the stop. **Document-derived, and compared:**
item, location, quantity by location, unit, source type, source id, **source
line** (with the transfer's `:out`/`:in` derivation), **posting role**, and
**effective date**, plus the movement count per line and the mirror case of a
movement with no posted document.

**Open, and reachable — a movement can diverge on these and still read
`consistent`:** `reason_code` / `reason_narrative`, and the movement's direct
transaction foreign key disagreeing with its line's transaction.

**Carried by a movement but NOT document-derived, so deliberately not compared:**
`recorded_at` (the posting instant, which legitimately differs from the header's),
`actor_id` (the posting actor, not a property of the document),
`source_revision` (the header's optimistic revision advances on the
draft-to-posted transition, so the two disagree by construction), and
`stock_dimension_set_version` (a command input with no header counterpart).
Comparing any of those would manufacture false discrepancies on correct data.

**The effective date is compared, added 2026-08-04** by the combined-tree review.
The posting path writes one `effectiveAt` to both the header and every movement
it produces, so a movement effective on a different day than its own document is
a posting defect — and a **balance-significant** one, because that date decides
which period the movement lands in and every as-of balance that contains it.
Without the comparison the arm reported such a line `consistent` while every
quantity agreed.

The posting path's existing check compares the **command** against the persisted
draft lines. That is not this check: it never re-reads the movements it wrote, and
it is gone by the time anything could go wrong afterwards.

### R1-b — sweep coverage

Every anchor in the scope is re-derived straight from the movement ledger with no
aggregate read involved, so a balance nobody looks at is verified anyway. The
sweep holds the **shared aggregate-generation guard** for its whole transaction:
every movement append advances the generation inside its own transaction and so
takes that key exclusively, which pins the ledger across both reads. Without it,
ordinary `READ COMMITTED` statement snapshots let a posting commit between the
document read and the movement read and produce a **false** discrepancy. The
operational cost is real and deliberate — while a scope reconciles, movement
appends in that tenant and environment wait.

- **Subjects** are anchors at the *current* movement generation. A superseded
  anchor is named in `excludedSubjects` with reason `supersededGeneration`, never
  silently dropped: it is provably unservable, because the cache key and
  `aggregateAnchorMatchesIdentity` both carry the generation and the generation
  only advances. **One exception, added 2026-08-04:** a superseded anchor that
  carries a *recorded* discrepancy is a discrepant subject rather than an
  exclusion. It is still never re-derived — it cannot be served — but burying it
  would hide every recorded discrepancy the moment the next posting advances the
  generation, which is to say almost always.
- Anchors of a query the registration does not name, or whose stored parameters
  do not match its declared shape, are **unverifiable**, not consistent.
- The registered query scopes itself with exactly one legal entity taken from its
  own operand, so an anchor whose stored `legal_entity_ids` disagrees with that
  operand answers a different question than its key claims. That is
  `AGGREGATE_ANCHOR_SCOPE_DIVERGED`, reported **discrepant**, and no sum is taken
  over either side because summing would launder the defect.
- A stored balance or ledger sum outside `numeric(38,18)` is
  `AGGREGATE_ANCHOR_BALANCE_UNRECOGNIZED` and unverifiable. The bound is checked
  on the **scaled magnitude**, not on digits written: a twenty-one digit integer
  carries no fraction yet still needs thirty-nine digits once scaled, so a
  "total digits" test would admit a value the column cannot hold — and migration
  `0020`'s `balance_value` check is lexical and does not see it either.

**What R1-b does NOT do, stated because a review round found it overstated by
omission.** The sweep re-derives the **balance** from the movement ledger. The
read path additionally verifies each anchor's `anchor_digest` against a recomputed
expected digest (`module-runtime-interpreter.ts:1277-1280`), and the sweep does
**not**. So a defective compute or restore can leave a current-generation anchor
whose balance and parameters are correct but whose digest is wrong: the sweep
reports it consistent, while the first ordinary read would detect and record it.
R1-b extends the balance half of the read-path check to unread anchors, not the
integrity half. Open, and not fixed here.

### R1-c — operator visibility

`renderInventoryReconciliationReport` produces deterministic operator-readable
lines: outcome, tenant/environment/legal entities, every count including the zero
ones, each consistent subject by name, and each finding with both disagreeing
values. The sweep also reads
`north_star_internal.semantic_aggregate_anchor_discrepancies` and re-surfaces the
read path's own recorded discrepancies as
`RECORDED_ANCHOR_DISCREPANCY_PRESERVED` — the first time anything in the system
shows that table's contents to a human.

**One surfacing gap remains open.** A *current-generation* anchor that returns
early — unrecognized query, unrecognized balance, unrecognized parameters,
diverged scope, or an unusable ledger sum — is reported under that code and never
reaches the recorded-discrepancy check, so a discrepancy recorded against such an
anchor stays hidden. The superseded-generation case was fixed; these branches
were not.

## Repairs nothing — structural, not intentional

The whole sweep runs inside `SET LOCAL transaction_read_only = on`. A repair is
refused by PostgreSQL rather than avoided by discipline, and the report carries
`transactionReadOnly` read back from `current_setting`, so an operator observes
the property instead of trusting it. `repairedSubjectCount` is typed `0`.

The arithmetic and the ledger re-derivation are re-expressed in this file rather
than reused from `inventory-posting-service.ts`: a verifier sharing a code path
with the thing it verifies cannot observe that path being wrong (AGENTS.md §6).

## ADR-0044 applied

Three outcomes, never two. `consistent` requires at least one subject and zero
unverifiable ones; a scope with no subjects, or with anything the reconciler
could not check, is `indeterminate`. Finding nothing and being unable to look
never render the same way.

**One control is a proxy and the packet says so.** The source-document arm reads
the documents and their movements in a **single statement**, so no writer can
split the pair. That property has no deterministic red available: reproducing an
interleaving between two statements would need a pause injected into production
code, and there is none. What *is* observed is the outcome the race would
produce — subtest 12 commits a legal transaction-line update that no movement
matches and asserts the arm reports `SOURCE_DOCUMENT_QUANTITY_DIVERGED`
(declared 8, observed 5) rather than calling the line consistent. The
single-statement read is verified by reading the code, not by a recorded red.

Tenant scoping has **no code victim in this file** and that is recorded rather
than papered over: separation is enforced by forced row-level security in
PostgreSQL, not by a line here. The control is therefore built to be decisive
instead — tenant B reuses tenant A's *legal-entity identifier*, so both scopes
name the same legal entity in the same physical table, and the test first proves
with an unscoped administrative query that both tenants' rows are there. Tenant
A's report then names none of tenant B's records and vice versa, and
`relrowsecurity`/`relforcerowsecurity` are asserted directly on the movement,
transaction, transaction-line and both anchor relations.

## Review history

**Round 1** (Codex `gpt-5.6-sol` xhigh, candidate `b45b06e2`) returned
**REVISE** with three findings, all confirmed against the code and all fixed:
the anchor's stored legal-entity scope was never compared with the query's own
operand; `parseStoredDecimal` accepted values outside `numeric(38,18)`; and the
sweep read a split snapshot. D1–D4 came back clean, including the
superseded-generation exclusion this packet depends on.

**Round 2** (candidate `4097cc39`) confirmed fix 2 exactly right and confirmed
that legitimate reads issue exactly one lowercased legal entity, that the
advisory key matches the SQL byte for byte, and that it introduces no lock-order
cycle. It returned **REVISE** on two ways the other two fixes were incomplete,
both confirmed and both fixed:

- **Membership was decided from the potentially corrupt stored scope**, before
  the comparison with the authoritative operand. An anchor with operand A and
  stored scope B was excluded from A's reconciliation as out of scope, so the
  check that would have named it never ran — and the round-1 control had masked
  this by reconciling A and B together. Membership now considers the stored
  scope **and** the operand, and the control now reconciles A alone.
- **The generation guard serializes movement appends but not document writes.**
  `inventory_transaction_line` is an ordinary updatable entity and its updates do
  not advance the generation, so two statements could still straddle a committed
  line update and report a diverged line consistent. The arm now reads both
  sides in **one statement**, which removes the race for every writer rather
  than for the one this module happens to know about.

**Round 3** (candidate `bbe5382a`) returned **BLOCK**. D2–D4 came back clean:
the single-statement snapshot faithfully preserves both queries it replaced,
read-only and tenant scoping are unchanged, and subtests 10 and 12 observe the
behaviours they claim. D1 found a **third instance of the same class**, and the
reviewer invoked the convergence rule itself rather than asking for another fix.

> `inventory-reconciliation-service.ts:550` — anchor membership /
> false-consistent result. Operand attribution depends on the all-or-nothing
> `recognizedAnchorParameters`. If a recognized query has a valid legal-entity
> operand A but another parameter is malformed, parsing returns `null`; an
> anchor whose corrupt stored scope names B is therefore excluded from
> reconciliation of A. This is reachable because storage constrains
> `parameter_values` only to be an object. Concrete scenario: stored scope
> `[B]`, valid legal-entity parameter A, invalid `atTime`, plus otherwise
> consistent A subjects. Reconciliation of A omits the anchor and can report
> `consistent` instead of including it as
> `AGGREGATE_ANCHOR_PARAMETERS_UNRECOGNIZED` and returning `indeterminate`.

**The lane agrees, and stops anyway.** The defect is real, material and
reachable on this packet's own premise — every anchor control here plants data a
defective compute path or a restore could leave. The fix is small: attribute the
legal-entity operand independently of whole-parameter recognition, so a
malformed `atTime` cannot discard a valid scope. It is not written here, because
`review-tiers` caps revisions at two and the charter says a third round on the
same **class** means stop and report. Three rounds on one question —
*how does a sweep attribute an anchor whose stored scope may itself be wrong?* —
is the signal the skill names: the charter is mis-scoped around that question,
and re-scoping it is the user's call, not a fourth continuation.

**What is decided by this, and what is not.** Everything outside anchor scope
attribution has been reviewed to clean across three rounds: the source-document
arm, the read-only property, ADR-0044's three outcomes, tenant scoping, the
decimal bound, the generation guard, and the single-statement snapshot. The open
finding narrows the sweep's coverage in one reachable case; it does not make any
reported finding wrong, and it cannot cause a repair.

One observation recorded rather than acted on: `inventory-posting-service.ts`
bounds its own decimals by total digits written, so it would accept a
twenty-one-digit integer and rely on the column to raise. That is the posting
service's business, not this packet's, and it fails closed at the database.

## Deliberate non-goals

- **No scheduler, no durable job.** Plan §11.6 build 10 stays deferred to G4.
- **No migration.** The reconciler reads only relations that already exist, so
  `0021` remains the last migration.
- **A movement whose transaction foreign key disagrees with its line's** is not
  checked. The binding for it was written and then removed rather than shipped
  unexercised; recorded as owed.
- **No HTTP or scheduled delivery of the report.** R1-c is the invocable report
  and its rendering; wiring it to an operator surface belongs with the durable
  job work.

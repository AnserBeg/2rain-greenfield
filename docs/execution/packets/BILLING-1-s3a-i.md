## Claims
1. B8. `bookingActuals` becomes `evidenceQuery {queryId, protocol: northstar.evidence:protocol.service_use_v1, subjectId}`. Billing names the protocol only. A provider registers, for its read-model binding, a decoder to the protocol snapshot, its own subject check and a cursor fence (`canonical-model/src/service-evidence.ts`, `postgres-provider/src/service-use-fence.ts`). Booking is the first provider, in its own files: the decoder and slot check in `canonical-model/src/booking.ts`, the fence in `booking-owner-usage.ts`. `billing-booking-usage.ts` is deleted. An unregistered binding is refused at compile time and at runtime (fail closed).
2. Replay rule. The reader-45 `bookingActuals` declaration reads as the protocol with its slot as subject (`billingEvidenceQuery`). Its bytes, plan digest, reader 45 and calculation v3 are unchanged. Three reviews (overrun 270.00, early return 90.00, a pause) reproduce the main-measured JSON byte for byte, digests included (`billing-booking-possession.test.ts`).
3. B1. A time component chooses `combination`: bestPrice (default, today's cover), ladder (whole blocks from the longest rate down; `capAtNextTier` caps the shorter rates at one more block of the rate above) or prorate (the longest rate one whole block of which fits, pro rata). `partial` per rate decides a final partial block. `cap` may be `{rate, times}`. At 20 a day or 100 a week, 3/10/13 days: prorate 60/142.86/185.71, ladder 60/160/220, capped ladder and bestPrice 60/160/200. Each rule applies per pricing window: 34 days across a month window cost 502.86 under prorate (31/7 weeks, then 3 days) and 520 under the others. Prorate ships "tier reached" only.
4. B7. `perQuantity` on a fixed fee multiplies by the service's one commercial quantity (5 seats x 15 = 75.00). `agreedInterval.quantityFieldId` binds a required integer, exact decimal or service-unit quantity field on the billed record; `agreedQuantity` reads it (1 when unbound, as before). Several quantity bands refuse.
5. B9. `evidenceQuery.earlyReturn {bill: agreedEnd | actualEnd, minimumTerm?: {period, count}}`. Measured on main first: absent keeps v3 (the agreed end closes the service and only use inside it is rated: agreed 5 days, returned on day 3 at 30 a day = 90.00, due day 5). actualEnd: 90.00 due day 3. agreedEnd: 150.00 due day 5. A one-week minimum term: 210.00 due day 7. Committed time runs from the LAST RETURN to the service end: picked up on day 1 instead, agreedEnd bills 120.00 and the one-week minimum 180.00 (time before pickup and gaps between legs are not committed; a minimum charge from the agreed start is s3a-ii scope with B2). Committed time is billed only after it elapses (arrears, so no B2). earlyReturn is rated under calculation v4 only. Separate from onEarlyEnd.
6. Any new key selects calculation v4 and reader 54; a plan with none keeps its bytes, version and floor. `calculate` refuses a rule key under another version and v4 without one. Billing parses every provider's snapshot against the protocol schema where it consumes it. The loader and projection billing terms moved into Billing's file (`billingReleaseTerms`, `billingReleaseMismatch`).
7. Permissions bind by declared role, not the `billing_quote` name: the quote grant is an active read of the billed record type that no query but a bound plan's quote uses (and not the source query's); the terms writes share one active create, update or transition grant on the terms record type that no operation but a bound plan's terms writes holds. Several bound plans may share one grant pair, as the old names forced.
8. The drafter renders `oneOf`, so it now sees every schedule, slot, component and combination variant (it saw `[value]`), and its guidance asks prorate or ladder. Nine kinds' lines changed (addRatePlanVersion, addRecordType, addField, addLineTable, addReportRevision, addMeasureRevision, addPopupActionRevision, addDateTimeField, saveWorkflow; the tool text grows from 15.7k to 20.2k characters), with no live eval.

## Rules 16, 17
(a) Service-use evidence is "when anything was actually used, by how much": rentals, timesheets, telematics hours, gate or room occupancy. A combination rule is "a measured quantity priced through ordered unit rates": equipment, storage, coworking, parking. A per-quantity fee is "rate x quantity": seats, desks, lockers. (b) Booking's query, a time clock or a meter read model can implement the protocol unchanged; any time component or fee takes the rules. (c) Narrower: one engagement per subject, one quantity per fee, the tier block not yet on meters (B6).

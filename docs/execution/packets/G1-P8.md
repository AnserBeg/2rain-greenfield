## Goal

Adopt the repository's `ux-grammar` draft as a loadable skill, pin its G1
contract to plan sections 8.5-8.6 and the accepted canonical/SurfaceRuntime
vocabulary, and make a browser surface outside the compiled issued-view seam
fail CI. This packet adds enforcement only; it changes no product behavior.

## Binding enforcement

1. The skill starts with valid top-level `name` and `description` frontmatter.
2. One versioned machine-readable block names plan sections 8.5-8.6, the five
   canonical archetypes and their slots, the four status roles, the exact G1
   component registry, the issued-view surface projection, and the sole
   `renderSurfaceRuntime` entrypoint.
3. The skill-pin check compares that block with the plan's binding anchors,
   canonical-model constants, P7 runtime parser, P7 closed registry, and the
   authenticated app-server composition. Any mismatch is a stable CI failure.
4. The surface-seam check permits structural browser markup only in
   SurfaceRuntime and its closed registry; only SurfaceRuntime may consume the
   surface contract/registry, and only the issued-view app server may invoke
   SurfaceRuntime. Direct release/pointer/filesystem/environment authority is
   rejected at the runtime seam.
5. Temporary-fixture negatives prove a displaced skill header, vocabulary
   drift, hardcoded screen, and out-of-vocabulary registered component all
   fail with stable rule IDs.

## Decisive review charter

Gates must be green on the frozen candidate. In scope:

- the skill-pin test genuinely fails when the skill and accepted code
  vocabulary differ;
- the surface-seam test genuinely fails for a hardcoded/bypass surface and an
  out-of-vocabulary registry entry;
- the real repository is green because every browser surface flows through
  the compiled issued-view SurfaceRuntime seam;
- no product surface or gateway source changed.

Threat model: accidental documentation drift and accidental surface bypass by
honest code. Out of scope: product surface behavior, gateways, G2 modules,
hostile-input or obfuscation resistance, and broader UX conformance.

Decisive questions:

- Does each induced mismatch make the scanner red for the intended stable
  rule, rather than merely testing a copied constant?
- Can a new ordinary screen emit structural markup outside SurfaceRuntime
  without a failing architecture test?
- Can the registry gain a component not present in the pinned vocabulary?
- Is the diff entirely mechanical and outside product source?

One fresh naive Codex `gpt-5.6-sol` xhigh review is required. Any correction
produces a new SHA and a fresh review; at most two REVISE rounds are allowed.
Fable is optional and not planned for this test-only packet.

# ADR-0083. Period and usage billing

Fable approved the slice-0 design with changes on 2026-11-20. This amendment incorporates R1–R13. The narrow re-read accepted the design with amendments A1 and A3–A7, incorporated here.

A customer should be able to price the use of a room, a service contract, a subscription or a metered item with the same capability. Billing owns the calculation. The source module owns the service facts. Receivables owns the invoice. Each can change independently because a charge identifies the exact versions of all three contracts that produced it.

Ship Billing as a versioned Tier B domain pack at customization ladder rung 6. Plan §2.3 excludes advanced pricing and multi-currency from initial launch. This is the reusable N3 commercial pricing capability (§12.6), with one currency per enrollment and invoice, Q2 Semantic Queries and O2 Semantic Operations (§12.12). Field service and rental industry packs belong to N6 (§12.9). A generated demonstration of reuse claims no industry-pack stage gate. An issued invoice is an operational customer document. It creates no journal entry, revenue recognition, inventory movement, cost relief or accounting balance. A future N6 ledger pack must separately admit posting, periods, reversal and reconciliation before it can treat an invoice as an accounting input. ## Which service is billable

A binding identifies a persisted record or owned line by stable type and ID. Its declared Semantic Queries return the commercial owner, customer, company, item if applicable, and a stable service identity shared by its intervals and usage observations. A source must provide an interval, metered usage or both. A usage-only service can group many log entries for one allowance. Two bindings to the same service and charge component cannot create two billing entitlements. Persist the service’s billing anchor once so that a form rename, reassignment of equipment, retry or release change cannot start another clock.

| Source evidence | Meaning at rating time |
| --- | --- |
| Interval | Half-open start and end instants, quantity and unit, with an explicit agreed or actual basis. Clip open actual use and open pauses to the captured as-of instant. An absent return does not authorize billing future actual time. |
| Booking | The amended ADR-0082 specifies `booking.ownerUsage/v1`. Slice 2 depends on BOOKING-1 implementing this owner-detail query. For each commercial owner record or line, consume its service and slot IDs, dated agreed intervals and quantities, actual-use legs with effective bounds and exact quantity/unit, assignment legs, and first-class pause intervals with IDs, revisions and explicit open ends. Select the declared agreed or actual basis. A swap keeps the same service. A hold, cancellation or free/busy answer alone proves no delivered service. [Booking read and event contracts](ADR-0082-booking-over-time.md#published-read-and-event-contracts) |
| Meter readings | Stable stream, unit, event ID/revision, instant and nonnegative exact cumulative value. Difference consecutive readings at required boundaries. Refuse a missing baseline, decrease/reset, conflicting observations or a gap across a required split. Ask for a corrected or boundary reading through the source operation. |
| Usage entries | Stable event IDs/revisions and nonnegative quantities in a declared unit. Attribute each increment at its effective instant. An entry exactly at the exclusive end belongs to the next window. Overlapping or ambiguous interval usage requires an exact split supplied by the source. |

A subscription period and a service-plan line use the same interval contract. Neither requires Booking or Inventory. The compiler resolves query IDs, field types, units, owner/line relations, read classifications and required permissions. Bind the queries for customer/list selection and calendar evidence as well. Execute only the declared queries under the acting principal, release, tenant, environment and stored company scope. A write consumes a complete versioned snapshot under the same transaction or verifies an equivalent source revision fence, including inserted or removed children. The source query must attest completeness for the specific service and horizon. A policy-filtered list cannot certify that no pauses exist. Partial visibility, truncated results, conflicting ownership or stale revisions refuse the whole draft. A read of a shared item or customer grants no access to another company’s charges.

For Booking, retain the owner scope, snapshot as-of and transactional owner event cursor. Incremental reads after that cursor must include late additions, supersessions and voids with their old and new effective bounds. Consume every page through the high-water cursor. Lock and compare the owner cursor when generating and advance the billing checkpoint with the committed effect. On mismatch reread. Due work includes any service whose source cursor is ahead of its billing checkpoint while it has issued coverage, including after enrollment end. `booking.changed/v1` or a cursor-comparison query raises it. Other source modules owe equivalent completeness and revision evidence. Booking owns correction of its facts. Billing owns the resulting commercial difference.

## The rate plan a business user publishes

A compiled plan version declares stable component IDs, one currency and unit/scale for every typed input slot, allowed rate periods, structural policies, schedule and source bindings. Price amounts live in effective-dated Tier B tables. A price change appends data without activating a release. Each quote pins both the structure version and the selected data versions. The compiler rejects unknown kinds, bases or stages, duplicate IDs, incompatible types, empty plans and unbounded settings. The operation publishing values verifies slot coverage, nonnegative exact amounts, scale, and minimum/cap compatibility. There is no tenant code, callback, loop or ambient clock. [Expression ceiling](ADR-0012-expression-kernel-ceiling.md)

A component is a discriminated union. The pair of **base reference** and **calculation stage** is checked for its kind. Every component has an explicit tax classification mapping.

| Kind | Base reference | Stage and admitted terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `time` | One bound service interval/quantity | `beforeMinimum`. Offer at most one price per unit for each of hour, day, week, four-week and month. Best cover feeds the duration/money minimum and time cap. |
| `fixed` | A service enrollment | `afterCapBeforeTax`. Choose `recurring` with a named calendar, anniversary or fixed-day cadence, or `oneOff` with one service-start or service-end occurrence. Each occurrence has a stable ID. |
| `meter` | One declared usage stream/unit | `afterCapBeforeTax`. Choose an explicit allowance basis and an overage price per excess unit. For `perBilledPeriod` also name the time component whose winning rate blocks earn allowance. |
| `percentage` (calculation v5) | One earlier component's line | `afterCapBeforeTax`. A rate from a percentage slot on one non-percentage base component, at its `gross` stage (after minimum, cap, proration and quantity) or `net` stage (after its discount); no default stage. It mirrors each base occurrence (`['pct', base occurrence]`) and its timing, rounded once on the exact base. Refused: another percentage, itself or an unknown ID as the base; a discount or timing on it. Tax is never a base. (BILLING-1 s3a-ii-b; this was the reserved row.) |

The ordered stages are raw time cover, minimum, cap, discount (calculation v5), other pre-tax charges including percentage components, rounded net, then platform tax. A user with the Billing terms grant chooses a classification for each component from the mapped platform tax codes or explicitly selects untaxed. The tax owner resolves the applicable code/rate under platform policy. A missing, unreadable or incompatible classification refuses generation. A tenant formula cannot set the tax or override the rounded net.

| Named policy | Default and alternatives |
| --- | --- |
| `pricingWindow` | One civil month, repeating from original service start. A cap selects a day, week, four-week or month period and establishes successive windows of that length from the same anchor. Window bounds are half-open. Every service has this clock, including one without a cap. A cycle boundary never opens a pricing window. |
| `minimumScope` | `perEnrollment` (default) or `perPricingWindow` for both the minimum duration and the minimum money per unit. Apply only for positive active time. A ceiling on the time subtotal can name a money slot or a period-rate slot. Fees and usage sit outside it. |
| `partialTime` | `wholeStarted` (default) or `prorateShortestTail`. A longer offer can cover the tail at full price. |
| `fixedFeeDuringPause` | `suspend` (default) or `continue` for recurring fees. Time always excludes the union of pauses. Usage excludes consumption in paused intervals and requires boundary readings or timed increments. A one-off follows its named event once. |
| `feeProration` and `allowanceProration` | Each selects `activeCoverage` (default) or `none`. Active coverage scales by eligible elapsed time divided by the full resolved basis interval. With `none`, any positive eligible coverage earns the full fee or allowance, zero earns zero. The full fixed fee can therefore fall due on each anniversary. The overage price and one-off amount are never prorated. |
| `allowanceBasis` | Required, with **no implicit default**. `perCycle` grants one quantity per invoice cycle. `perPricingWindow` grants one per service-anchored window. `perBilledPeriod` specifies an included quantity for each offered period of the referenced time component (for example, hours included per day or week). For the first two bases, unused units expire at their boundary. For billed periods the block grants pool only within their pricing window and expire when it closes. |

**Amounts and selection.** One compatible whole table supplies time prices, minima, ceiling, fixed fees, allowance quantities and overage prices. Select customer override, then the highest-priority applicable active assigned price-list override, then the base table. Resolve item attachments before type defaults. Conflicting matches at equal rank refuse. Evaluate effective dates at the opening of the component’s occurrence (the pricing window for time). Until the first allocation, select from publications visible in the quote snapshot, including publications made after opening. At first allocation, retain the exact table and assignment for that opening. Later allocations reuse those terms even when a successor is backdated. A billed-period allowance shares the time table’s window terms. Each enrollment pins its service start, zone/calendar, service units and one of CAD, USD or EUR. A multi-currency customer uses separate enrollments. Mixed currency, incompatible units or unrepresentable mappings refuse compilation. A data-writing operation checks values against the same compiled types. No currency conversion occurs. ## How a line earns its amount

1. Capture the plan, terms, release, algorithm, source snapshot and calendar. Build dated quantity bands from disjoint actual evidence, subtracting the union of pauses for each service quantity. Adjoining assignment legs preserve its prior coverage. For a partial return, carry surviving units forward at the original anchor. If fungible quantity legs cannot uniquely allocate previously billed minimum, cap or usage, v1 refuses that occurrence and identifies the missing allocation.
2. Measure an hour as 3,600 elapsed seconds. A day, week and four-week block span one, seven and 28 **local days** in the pinned IANA zone, at the service anchor’s wall time. A local day can contain 23 or 25 hours. Number the days from service start. Within each day measure the active fraction by eligible elapsed duration divided by the actual length of that anchor-to-anchor day. Sum these fractions for civil offers and use elapsed seconds for hourly offers. A month offer covers the number of civil days between this pricing window’s start and its next local monthly anniversary. For monthly windows clamp each occurrence from the **original** day independently (Jan 31 → Feb 28 → Mar 31 in a common year). In a four-week window use the month measured from that window’s own start. Pin the resolved boundaries and offsets. A missing local anchor time resolves to the first valid instant after the gap. An ambiguous one resolves to the earlier offset. Pin both rules in the calculation version.
3. For each pricing window, find the least exact price covering the **entire active prefix** through the as-of boundary. For mixed offers each candidate consumes successive active coverage from the same ordered timeline, in its declared civil or elapsed measure. Include mixed-period covers and a longer block that covers the tail. Under `prorateShortestTail`, only the last block of the shortest offered period may be fractional. Break equal prices by fewer blocks, then more of the first differing count in descending period size (month first on a tie), then that same order of blocks. This fixes the winning blocks and their allowance attribution. Bound input sizes, candidate states and numeric precision in the calculation version. Exceeding a limit refuses the whole quote before mutation.
4. An entirely paused window earns zero time and triggers no minimum. For positive active time, the time floor is the higher of the entered money minimum and the best-cover price of the minimum duration from that window’s anchor. For `perPricingWindow`, take the greater of earned time and the floor, apply the ceiling, then multiply by the applicable quantity. For `perEnrollment`, value the floor once under the first positive window’s pinned terms and ceiling. Compare that floor to the running sum of window-capped earnings. Subsequent earnings absorb the floor before further time becomes due. A minimum duration must fit its window. Retain the rounded cumulative entitlement per commercial quantity/component and subtract **prior allocated plus reserved cents** at each invoice boundary, including zero. A schedule change cannot reset this calculation.
5. Rate fixed and meter occurrences on their selected basis. For `perBilledPeriod`, use only the final winning blocks of the pricing window. Prorate each block’s allowance by the active portion it covers when configured, sum those allowances and compare to the stream’s eligible window usage once. A minimum or cap changes money and earns no additional allowance blocks. For a usage-only service, the enrollment supplies effective coverage dates. Overage is `max(0, eligible usage − earned allowance) × overage rate`. Draft overage only when its basis closes (or service ends with final evidence), so a weekly offer replacing daily blocks cannot retrospectively increase the allowance on already invoiced interim usage. Recurring fees settle at their own cadence at their declared timing (arrears unless the plan states advance, calculation v5), including a final partial occurrence. All proration uses exact fractions. On an early close retain the full scheduled denominator for the fraction.
6. Compare unrounded candidates using integer decimal coefficients and exact rational fractions. Round the final cumulative net half-up to cents once per charge line before allocating the difference. **Billing’s rounded net is authoritative** on a billing-origin invoice line. Include first-class `servicePeriodStart` and exclusive `servicePeriodEnd`, charge/occurrence/revision, currency, tax classification and derivation reference. Use the covered interval for fees and usage, including the opening service period for a one-off start fee and the last delivered period for a one-off end fee. Display quantity, selected rates and discount evidence for explanation. Receivables applies platform tax to the rounded net and sums the rounded lines. Its order-line `lineAmounts()` multiplication cannot reconstruct a minimum, cap or mixed-period price. [Commercial arithmetic](../../packages/postgres-provider/src/commercial-amounts.ts)
7. Keep recoverable inputs and their canonical digest, source IDs/revisions/cursor, agreed/actual basis, active and excluded spans, quantity bands, both clocks, resolved terms and assignments, ordered winning blocks, minimum/cap, allowance/usage, fractions, rounding, tax evidence, prior allocations and result digest. The explanation query shows that derivation under both source and invoice permissions. A replay uses the pinned versions.

## When a draft is due

The compiled schedule is the closed union `calendarMonth | anniversary(n months) | fixedDays(n) | onServiceEnd`. Positive bounded integers admit quarterly/annual anniversaries and 28-day cycles. Calendar months start at the first date’s business-day boundary in the pinned calendar. Anniversary and fixed-day cycles repeat from the original schedule anchor in local time, using the same clamping and boundary-resolution rules. Service-end generation requires a source-attested end. For a per-cycle allowance on that schedule use the resolved service start/end as the denominator. A recurring fixed component always declares its own finite cadence.

**Timing per component (calculation v5).** Arrears is the default and the only timing before v5: a cycle drafts delivered coverage through its exclusive end. Agreed service contract terms may establish earned coverage after it elapses. An actual-use source needs actual evidence. Missing final readings keep affected work visibly pending. Since s3a-ii a recurring fixed fee may bill in advance under `keepPaidPeriod`, which never returns value, so it needs no credit remedy (see below). `prorateAndRefund` still waits for a separately admitted Receivables credit-on-account or refund operation that can return all unearned value even after payment. A timer, approved future booking or advance estimate cannot produce an advance invoice for anything not admitted below.

**Booking actuals under calculation v3** (BILLING-1 slice 2 fix round, 2026-11-22):

- **Precision.** Booking stamps start, return, pause, resume and swap with its decision clock, in microseconds. Billing truncates every Booking instant toward the past to the millisecond before rating it. Truncation preserves order, so Booking's no-overlap and pause-inside-use rules still hold. A span shorter than a millisecond becomes empty and is accepted. Billing never refuses a Booking instant for its precision.
- **Possession sets the service end (owner ruling, 2026-11-22).** Under `bookingActuals`, billing follows actual possession. Billing reads possession through the review instant. Cycles continue while a use leg is live past the agreed end. An open leg past the agreed end bills the review instant's completed cycles, as for any live hire. Once every leg is returned, the final cycle ends at the recorded return. For example, a hire agreed for five days and returned on day nine bills nine days: CAD 270.00 at CAD 30 a day.
- **Early return is unchanged for now.** A return before the agreed end keeps the agreed end, and the possession inside it is rated. The orchestrator ruled on 2026-11-22 that early return is a general plan choice, not one fixed rule. A later slice adds a declared plan option: "bill to agreed end" or "bill to actual return", with an optional minimum term, because rentals, storage and subscriptions differ.
- **Reader.** A plan with an arrears source compiles at surface rung 45. Rungs 42 to 44 belong to REPORTS-1. Older readers refuse the release by name.
- **No use.** A hire with no use leg keeps the agreed end. A review that falls due without actual use is refused. A second booking for the same owner in the slot, inside the review window, is also refused: the review needs exactly one tracked booking.

**Rate rules and service-use evidence under calculation v4** (BILLING-1 slice 3a-i, 2026-11-26):

- **Service-use evidence.** `evidenceQuery` names a query whose read model implements `northstar.evidence:protocol.service_use_v1`: one owner's engagements (a provider subject, such as a Booking slot), each with use legs (half-open, exact quantity and unit) and pauses, complete through `asOf` at a cursor. A provider registers a decoder, its own subject check and a cursor fence; an unregistered binding is refused. Booking is the first provider, in its own files. Billing names the protocol, never the provider. **Replay rule:** the `bookingActuals` declaration persisted at reader 45 reads as the protocol with its slot as the subject; its bytes, digest, reader 45 and calculation v3 are unchanged.
- **Combination.** `bestPrice` (default: the least exact price of any cover), `ladder` (whole blocks from the longest rate down, the remainder in started blocks of the shortest; `capAtNextTier` caps the shorter rates at one more block of the rate above) or `prorate` (the longest rate one whole block of which fits, pro rata for the whole active time). `partial` per rate decides a final partial block: whole by default, pro rata under `prorate`. A cap may be `{rate, times}`. At CAD 20 a day or 100 a week, 3, 10 and 13 days cost 60, 142.86, 185.71 (prorate), 60, 160, 220 (ladder) and 60, 160, 200 (capped ladder, bestPrice).
- **Quantity.** `perQuantity` on a fixed fee multiplies by the service's one commercial quantity. `agreedInterval.quantityFieldId` binds a required exact number on the billed record as that quantity (1 when absent). Several quantity bands refuse.
- **Early return** (`evidenceQuery.earlyReturn`, separate from any onEarlyEnd policy). Absent keeps calculation v3: the agreed end closes the service and only use inside it is rated (agreed five days, returned on day three at CAD 30: CAD 90 due on day five). `actualEnd` ends the service at the last return (CAD 90 due on day three). `agreedEnd` keeps the service open to the agreed end and bills the committed time from the LAST RETURN to the service end (picked up at the start: CAD 150 due on day five; picked up on day one: CAD 120). `minimumTerm {period, count}` from service start holds either open until it elapses, committed from the last return (one week: CAD 210 picked up at the start, CAD 180 picked up on day one; due on day seven). Time before the first pickup and gaps between legs are not committed: a minimum charge from the agreed start is new scope (s3a-ii, with B2). Committed time is billed only after it has elapsed, so this stays arrears.
- **Versioning.** Any of these keys selects calculation v4 and the rules reader; a plan with none keeps its bytes, calculation and floor.
- **Permissions** bind by declared role: the quote grant is an active read of the billed record type, distinct from the source query's grant; the three terms writes share one active create, update or transition grant on the terms record type.

**Per-component timing under calculation v5** (BILLING-1 slice 3a-ii-a, 2026-11-28; owner ruling B2, delegated):

- **Key.** `timing {when: advance | arrears, earlyEnd?: keepPaidPeriod | prorateAndRefund}` on `time` and on a recurring `fixed` component. Absent means arrears, so v2 to v4 plans replay byte for byte. There is no implicit timing under v5: a plan that states timing on any component states it on every time and recurring fixed component, or it is refused. Meters and one-off fees take no timing (usage, overage, fuel, damage and one-offs bill in arrears). Any timing key selects calculation v5 and the timing reader; v5 also rates every v4 rule key.
- **Admitted in this slice.** Only a recurring fixed fee bills in advance. Refused: advance on a time component (slice e), a meter or a one-off; advance with `fixedFeeDuringPause` absent or `suspend` (suspending would need a refund, and absent means suspend); `prorateAndRefund`; an `earlyEnd` on an arrears component (declared and never read); advance on a periodMeter plan or on a plan with no interval or service-use source. **Advance on service use requires a declared `earlyReturn`** (orchestrator ruling, 2026-11-28, on the pre-merge review): without one, service use holds the service open to the agreed end, so cycles after a return would keep opening at the full fee with no declared commitment (agreed Jun 10 to Aug 31, returned Jul 12: August 300.00). So the persisted `bookingActuals` form and an `evidenceQuery` without `earlyReturn` refuse advance; `actualEnd` stops cycles at the return and `agreedEnd` is a declared commitment.
- **Owed cycles.** At a review, every cycle of the fee's cadence whose start (clamped to the service anchor) is at or before the review instant and before the service end is owed. A late first review owes every opened cycle; no cycle opens at or after the service end. An advance-only review (no cycle closed yet) rates only advance fees.
- **Amount.** Each owed cycle is rated over its whole span, never clipped to the review instant, on the agreed interval (anchor to the agreed end, kept as evidence `agreedEnd` before actual use moves the service end) together with any actual use through the review instant. CAD 300 a month from Jun 10: June 210.00 (21/30), July 300.00. The amount does not change when the cycle closes.
- **keepPaidPeriod** (the default early end). An early end never reduces an opened cycle: returned Jul 12 under `actualEnd`, July stays 300.00 and August never opens (total 510.00).
- **Correction, not early end.** On an agreed-interval source the record's end field is both the agreed and the service end, so shortening it is a correction (see the correction rule below): the reviews re-rate on the corrected interval. An early end that keeps the paid period needs a source whose actual end differs from the agreed end (service-use evidence).
- **Overrun (orchestrator ruling, delegated, 2026-11-28).** Use past the agreed end is billed in addition to the agreed coverage, once: coverage is the union of the agreed interval and actual use, so agreed days are never counted twice. An overrun cycle's amount grows as the review instant advances (agreed to Jul 15, still out on Aug 10: July whole, August 9/31 so far).
- **Unruled choice, recorded for the owner.** keepPaidPeriod bills an opened cycle even if no review saw it before the return (the ruling said "the period already billed"). It may be overridden.
- **Statelessness.** Reviews read no prior allocations yet. A negative advance difference, and the credit hold on advance Generate, are s3b gates (drafts and coverage land on the open-item core).

**Minimum terms, discounts and percentage components under calculation v5** (BILLING-1 slice 3a-ii-b, 2026-11-28; owner rulings B2 and B13, delegated). Any of these keys selects calculation v5 and the pricing reader (its own rung: the timing rung admits timing only). Any v5 key, not only a timing key, requires stated timing on every time and recurring fixed component.

- **The minimum term is a minimum charge from the agreed start** (orchestrator ruling, 2026-11-28: charge exactly the term from the agreed start, prorated, never more). On `time` it is `minimumDuration` with `perEnrollment` (the default): the floor is priced from the first positive pricing window, which is anchored at the agreed start, and it is owed at the actual end. Agreed seven days at CAD 30, minimum seven days, returned on day three under `actualEnd`: CAD 210.00 due on day three (v4's early-return `minimumTerm` made it due on day seven); picked up on day one, still CAD 210.00. Under v5 a minimum duration that can never fit its pricing window refuses at parse; month lengths decide the remaining cases at rating.
- **Fee minimum term.** A recurring `fixed` fee may declare `minimumTerm {period, count}` from the agreed start. When the service ends before the term end, the fee for [service end, term end) falls due at the service end, under the natural cycle identities; the cycle that crosses the term end is prorated to it. CAD 300 a month from Jun 10, three-month term, returned Jul 12: Jun 210.00, Jul 300.00, Aug 300.00, Sep 1-10 90.00, total 900.00 = 3 x 300. In advance the held cycles are rated on the term alone, never on an open-ended agreed interval; in arrears the review that reaches the service end rates the cycles to the term end. A one-off fee takes no term. `feeProration: none` refuses beside either term form on a fee (it would bill a partly held cycle whole: September 300.00 instead of 90.00). A fee term longer than the calendar horizon (3,660 days) fails closed at review (owed: a parse refusal). A plan that states both the fee term and `earlyReturn.minimumTerm` refuses (two forms for one rule); the early-return form stays admitted on its own, because it is time's only minimum longer than one pricing window.
- **Correction to slice a (orchestrator ruling, 2026-11-28).** An advance fee whose service end is held past the last return by `earlyReturn` (its minimum term, or `agreedEnd`) billed every held cycle that opened before the held end whole on an open-ended agreed interval: the same facts with `earlyReturn.minimumTerm {month, 3}` billed a whole September, CAD 1,110.00. A cycle that opens after the last return is owed only because of the hold, so it is now rated on the committed interval (last return to the held end) alone: CAD 900.00, as for the fee term. That form keeps its timing (held cycles fall due as they open). A fee term still binds on a cycle that opens between the return and a held agreed end (pre-merge review). **Ruled (orchestrator, delegated, 2026-11-28):** a minimum term only adds cycles that would otherwise not open; it never shrinks a cycle already opened and billed in advance. A return inside the term's last cycle on an open-ended agreement keeps that cycle whole under keepPaidPeriod, with or without a term (E1m returned Sep 5: CAD 1,110.00).
- **Discount (B13).** `discount {kind: percent, rate: <percentage slot>, reason}` on `time`, `fixed` and `meter` reduces that line's base after the minimum and the cap, before tax. The line's entitlement is the net, rounded once; the line carries `discount {kind, reason, rate, rateVersion, grossExact, discountExact, grossCents, discountCents}` with `discountCents = grossCents - netCents`, so gross = net + discount exactly in cents. Time discounts each pricing window's capped amount at that window's rate and the enrollment floor at the first positive window's rate, then multiplies by quantity; its rates are in the window derivations. Cover selection runs on undiscounted prices (a percentage scales every candidate equally). A meter's allowance is never discounted. CAD 30 a day or 100 a week, four-week window, cap 240, 10% off: CAD 90.00 after seven days (gross 100), CAD 216.00 after twenty (gross 240). **Unruled choice, recorded for the owner:** the discount applies after the minimum, so a discounted line can fall below the stated minimum charge. Amount discounts are not admitted yet.
- **Percentage slots.** `{kind: percentage, scale <= 4}` beside money and quantity. `publishRateTable`, the validator every publish, override and quote shares, refuses a value above 100, so no override can produce a negative net; 100 is a whole discount (a 0.00 line keeping its evidence). Per-customer and price-list discounts use the ordinary override precedence.
- **Percentage component.** See the component table. A 12% waiver on the discounted time line above: CAD 25.92 on `net`, 28.80 on `gross`; split 10.80 then 15.12. On an advance fee it is billed with each opened cycle. Its rate is read at its base line's opening (for time, the first positive pricing window, as for the enrollment floor), so a rate change mid-service does not re-rate the cumulative time line's percentage (deliberately narrower).
- **Not yet admitted:** time billed in advance (slice e) and amount discounts (slice f).

**Cutover** records the first unbilled instant, old-process evidence and original service and schedule anchors. Carry prior cumulative entitlements, allocated cents, minimum/cap and allowance consumption, source cursor and necessary boundary readings, even mid-window at an otherwise clean invoice date. Refuse unknown coverage or overlapping enrollments. Persist zero-charge coverage and consume due occurrences in order. A preview range has no effect on those clocks.

Refresh an open draft by superseding its revision. After issue, a later source correction produces a linked supplemental draft for a positive difference or a Receivables credit proposal for a reduction, under the original rates and tax evidence. Never mutate an issued line. For example, an issued CAD 160 net corrected to CAD 130 at zero tax proposes a CAD 30 credit. A settled invoice may have insufficient creditable balance. Retain the unapplied difference as a visible unresolved correction until it can be settled through an admitted Receivables remedy. A released occurrence keeps its allocated cents reserved to that occurrence until its successor is issued or explicitly closed. Later occurrences compute against allocated plus reserved cents. Generation of a later occurrence never absorbs a released one. Refuse void while an issued supplemental links to the invoice. Void the dependent first. An applied credit already blocks void under the wholly unsettled rule. Rate the reissue under the original pinned terms on current evidence. Manual adjustment lines are not carried to the reissue unless re-entered. The Receivables credit resulting from a billing correction is confirmation-class, bound to the invoice line and correction digest. Order-origin posting keeps its v1 decision semantics.

## Compose it in Customize

An author attaches a plan to a record type or item, selects an interval or meter Semantic Query for the billable line, and binds **Generate charges** and **Issue invoice** to their released operations. Separate the structural diff in Customize from governed price and service records in Operate.

| Owner | How the definition or data changes |
| --- | --- |
| Compiled release | The add-only members are `addRatePlanVersion` (structure, slots and policies), `addBillingSchedule`, `addBillableSource`, `addRatePlanAttachment` and `addAttachmentEnd` (references the attachment and its prospective effective end). Supersession appends an end and successor in one patch. Each has a stable ID, closed payload, references and `schemaVersion`. Compile them atomically in dependency order. Reject changed ID reuse, ambiguous targets, unsupported query shapes or weakened confirmation. Historical releases retain the old members. |
| Versioned Tier B data | Only Billing’s `publishRateTable`, `setRateOverride`, `assignBillingPriceList`, `enrollService` and `changeEnrollmentSchedule` write those respective effective-dated facts. Each captures scope, expected revision, actor, idempotency and immutable version. `endRateOverride`, `endBillingPriceListAssignment` and `endEnrollment` append effective ends. Replacement appends a linked successor. Superseding terms or ending an attachment preserves old coverage and pinned terms. Schedule change carries all open pricing windows through a reviewed prospective cutover. Generic CRUD, rules, workflow computations and imports cannot write these facts. |

For “equipment rental with daily, weekly and monthly rates and a 28-day cap,” the AI drafter proposes one bundle with record types, BOOKING-1’s bookable resource and owned line, rate-plan structure, four-week window and cap reference, schedule, attachment, billable query and both actions. Verification, approval, activation, receipts and rollback use the ordinary release and operation paths. The same members bind a standalone usage stream or a service-contract period.

Each executable capability slice takes its own next scalar reader rung at merge on freshly fetched main. Rungs are never reserved or shared across slices. Regenerate artifacts and tests, exercise refusal by the real preceding reader, reproduce omitted-feature bytes for first-party and tenant patches, and re-fetch before pushing. Deployments honor the highest patch-parser and surface-reader floors already used by any tenant.

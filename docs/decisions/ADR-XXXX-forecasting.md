This proposal follows the amended design at `65bdc9f1` and depends on its implementation for custom models. `XXXX` is a placeholder. BOOT-CHECK, PLANNER-1, ANALYZE-1 and COMMS-2 each claimed 0090. Assign the next free ADR number against freshly fetched `origin/main` at merge and update the filename, heading, links and ledger row. A controller wants to know whether payroll in week three takes the company below its cash buffer. A buyer wants to know which yard will run out of a part before a confirmed receipt. A rental manager wants to compare the capacity already committed next week with what the fleet can serve. In **Customize → Forecasts** an authorized owner chooses a supplied method, reviews its sources and defaults, and publishes it. Staff run it from the declared company surface and can see which assumptions produced the estimate.

The rentals ERP survey and its read-only `public/collections.html` and `backend/cashForecast.js` inform the 13-week view, the low point, and the review of payroll and recurring costs. They also show the risk of adding an “open” invoice to a bank balance that already includes its payment. Forecasters in this ERP use its own governed facts. Rain stays a separate product. ## 1. Register three standard calculations

Propose commissioning a forecasting **Q2** family through plan §§5.9.1 and 5.11. Register Cash, Demand and stock-out, and Rental utilisation as three named, versioned, bounded calculations. Each registry entry publishes its accepted input roles and contracts, scope and grain, calendar, output schema, supported assumptions, method version and work limits. Each role resolves to a specific governed Semantic Query in the release. REPORTS-1 remains the catalog for observed facts and declared measures. Its eligible queries and domain read models can supply inputs if they prove the required grain, time horizon and contributor lineage. A missing input contract leaves that calculation unavailable for that company. [Plan §§5.9, 5.11, 12.8 and 12.9](../greenfield-north-star-erp-platform-plan.md) · [ADR-0089](ADR-0089-declared-reports.md)

The Q2 handler reads typed facts and calculates a typed **Forecast estimate**. It has no write effects. Ordinary named Semantic Operations handle run, cancel, save and archive, using the existing O0/O1 lifecycle and job contracts. The worker’s only publication effect is the forecast artifact and its receipt. Custom Python remains an ANALYZE-1 run (§6). This keeps provider authority and forecast semantics inspectable. Arbitrary joins would lose the declared grain, a Q3 Python query would turn custom output into a data source, and O2/O3 posting hooks would put an estimate on a business write path. Forecast estimates have evidence-only sinks and cannot bind to a report measure, query source, formula, workflow input, connector, posting operation, or authoritative dependency set. Any later human action goes through §6. [Plan §10.6](../greenfield-north-star-erp-platform-plan.md#106-the-customization-authority-boundary)

**Cash coverage.** Use the current business-day opening as the cash cut and show it on the result. Require each source watermark to cover that cut, and require each query to reconstruct facts known at the cut. Exclude later receipts from both the opening and the settled side of the residuals. Withhold if the source cannot reconstruct the cut, if currency is unresolved or if a required obligation lacks a date. Cash v1 covers one currency and one complete declared cash pool. The source provides that currency and pool membership. A known payroll or recurring cost with a posted payable is represented by the payable alone, using a governed occurrence association. An unresolved overlap blocks that scenario until a person excludes the manual line or the source proves the association. Payroll and recurring amounts are explicit scenario estimates. Mixed currencies yield an uninterpretable total, mismatched cuts can count one receipt twice, and inferred payroll can place the dip in the wrong week. For example, a $50,000 opening plus $12,000 expected receipts, less $8,000 bills and $20,000 payroll, gives a $34,000 close. After the receipt clears, a later cut has a $62,000 opening and zero residual for that invoice. With the same outflows, the close is still $34,000. Changing the payroll date moves the dip in a new scenario.

**Stock coverage.** Score one supported stock key at a time in v1. Refuse unresolved locations, base-unit mismatches, unknown dates for scoped commitments/inbound, and incomplete history. A confirmed overdue outbound remainder is due on the first day. An overdue inbound promise requires an updated expected date. Forecast 28 days by default (7–91 days). Show projected shortfall and at-risk dates as daily closing estimates. Label the history “fulfilled demand”. Lost sales and substitution are outside the model. With 18 units on hand, a day-one baseline of six and eight due gives a close of 10. A day-two baseline of six and seven confirmed inbound brings the close to 11. Starting from available stock or adding commitments to baseline would count claims twice. Pooling yards would hide local shortages. [ADR-0016](ADR-0016-stock-identity-dimension-set.md)

**Rental coverage.** Start with serialized, company-owned fleets and confirmed bookings. Run 28 days by default (7–91 days). Forecast active rentals to the declared expected return, adjusted only by the reviewed scenario. Show a distinct coverage gap for unresolved returns. Exclude quote conversion, shared pools and third-party re-rent from v1. Use an authoritative per-unit serving allocation. If the booking pack cannot prove an assignment, show the unresolved demand as capacity pressure with no contribution to occupied hours. A four-unit, 24-hour day with one unit down all day and 36 occupied unit-hours yields 36/72, or 50%. A fleet count obscures maintenance and different interval lengths. Quoted work would inflate committed use. A capped percentage alone would conceal overcommitment. [ADR-0082](ADR-0082-booking-over-time.md)

**Time and values.** Start at the working company’s captured business-day boundary. Use half-open intervals and adjacent civil dates, with 13 consecutive seven-date buckets from that boundary for cash. Resolve each boundary in its recorded IANA zone, applying the admitted gap/overlap rule and recording calendar, rule and tzdb pins. Rental interval weights come from elapsed time between the resolved instants. Cash daily lows make no claim about intraday ordering. Use signed exact decimal money and stock units, exact ratios for utilisation and model averages, and an explicit display rounding rule. Hold the scope, unit and method fixed in each comparison. Browser time and fixed 24-hour shifts can place a fact in the wrong date. Floating-point currency can introduce residual differences. ## 2. Give each set of assumptions a version people can compare

**Customize** publishes one add-only `addForecastRevision` member with a closed, versioned union for the three calculation kinds. Use stable forecast, assumption and revision IDs, an expected predecessor, a chosen method/version, typed query/field selections with scope/grain/time/unit contracts, an authoritative company-record binding, an allowed output surface binding, an optional governed Task binding and a default scenario. Defaults are explicit and reviewable. Active/retired is a closed variant; a retired revision preserves identity and carries no live bindings. The compiler resolves IDs, types, ranges, precision, source purpose and declared company scope. Labels resolve at render time. First-party starter settings and in-app authors use this same path.

A company operator uses a **Run forecast** Task to choose eligible inputs and edit an allowed scenario privately. The shape includes typed offsets, thresholds, lookback and horizon, and cash cost lines with currency supplied by the cash pool and an explicit one-time or finite recurrence rule. Cash cost occurrences use nonnegative amounts, effective date bounds and stable IDs. A recurring run expands only dates in the displayed horizon. The Task previews normalized values, sources, skipped optional inputs and what changed from the published defaults. Pin a run to the exact scenario values and digest. Changing a scenario produces a new run, while making it the company default requires a new reviewed release revision through Customize. Each company may keep several named forecast definitions. Customize uses the actual company record and its publication grant, and the compiler prohibits cross-company source or surface bindings. This leaves Tuesday’s forecast legible after Friday’s assumptions change. Global settings would overwrite the context of existing forecasts. Free-form formulas or learned defaults would obscure what the owner approved. [ADR-0079](ADR-0079-form-and-record-composition.md)

## 3. Capture once, publish once, reauthorize each read

The gateway supplies the acting person, active company, tenant/environment and verified release root/fence. Resolve entity ownership and identity from persisted records. Request parameters only narrow the authorized query range. Read every source and contributor in one bounded read-only snapshot at a comparable cut, retaining canonical source IDs/contracts, normalized inputs, read and recorded horizons, contributor IDs and revisions, and all field dependencies, including filters and grouping. Derived aggregates need underlying lineage. Before a number is shown, prove complete coverage of the requested population under the person’s company, row, field and classification grants. A missing field or denied row yields the same generic unavailable response as other unavailable sources, with no hidden value, count or label. Freshness and incompleteness can have named explanations when the person can see the affected sources. Connector-fed facts are eligible only through their admitted stored query with a complete watermark. A run never performs a provider refresh or uses connector credentials. Admin reads or connector credentials would exceed the person’s grants. A partial company total could look valid while a bill is missing. [ADR-0071](ADR-0071-read-decisions-are-live-per-request-writes-per-call.md) · [ADR-0080](ADR-0080-connector-framework.md) · [ADR-0089](ADR-0089-declared-reports.md)

Proposed standard-run ceilings are 10,000 source facts, 8 MiB of canonical inputs, 4 MiB of lineage, 500 output rows and 2 MiB of output per run, with stricter source scan limits and a five-second monotonic capture deadline. Backtests have the aggregate cap in §5. Bound visited rows and bytes at the gateway, including a cap-plus-one probe, and refuse the whole run on excess. Allow one active run per person and at most two per tenant/environment, with a lower limit when host capacity requires. Cap pending runs at four per tenant/environment and expire them after a two-minute wait. Reserve the slot and bytes atomically before work. Every read or publication recheck has a five-second monotonic budget. Enforce a 30-second execution deadline, cancel queued and running work, and check cancellation, live policy and the serving fence before handing off a capture or publishing a result. An operator or tenant stop prevents new starts and late publication. A worker cannot start as a privileged substitute for the person. Truncation could silently skew the forecast. Unbounded scans or queues let one company stall other work.

An immutable **Forecast estimate** records kind, scope, actor, release/fence, method version, assumption revision and exact values, query contracts and watermarks, input and output digests, coverage and exclusions, and typed period rows and summary. It is available through its admitted evidence List and Record. An explicit idempotent **Save forecast** can retain one private version and audit event from an authorized completed run. Recheck current source access for every run, list, search, detail, page or controlled handoff, including archived source reads where allowed. Use one scoped set-based check per source under a request budget. Exhaustion or a purged/unverifiable contributor withholds the version and its metadata. Recheck before release of bytes. Saved evidence lives in company-scoped provider stores (`forecast_runs`, `forecast_run_lineage` and immutable `forecast_versions`), with no raw retained snapshots or general file upload. Trials expire within 24 hours, earlier when the separate 20 MiB tenant/environment staging cap evicts the oldest completed trial. A new run that cannot reserve space after eligible eviction fails before capture. Bound saved estimates to a separate evidence quota (proposed 50 MiB per tenant/environment including lineage), initially private to the author. Attach a default 90-day retention class and apply the governed expiry, hold, purge and tombstone rules. Release retirement prevents fresh runs and leaves retained evidence subject to current access. Replacing a version would erase the forecast someone relied on. Link access alone could expose facts after a role change. [ADR-0078](ADR-0078-governed-documents-files-templates-and-signatures.md)

Store the exact decision and idempotency receipt against actor, scope, release fence and digest. A shared release’s company B run cannot access company A’s data. Writes, including cancel, archive and expiry, use scoped functions with `search_path = pg_catalog, pg_temp` and no direct runtime table grants. Use isolation guards wherever the writer relies on READ COMMITTED. Use one lock order for the tenant/environment quota, person run slot, run and version, including recovery and sweep. System job and expiry keys occupy a separate keyspace from user requests. Atomically couple each committed artifact and audit receipt. This makes a retried submit return the same outcome under fresh authority. A broad background role could cross the company boundary. Writing audit separately could leave a published artifact without its receipt.

## 5. Backtest against what people could have known

Publish an add-only `addForecastBacktestRevision` with stable ID, revision, predecessor, active/retired variants, standard forecast ID and compatible method/configuration contract, historical input and actual query IDs and contracts, and scoring version. Mark it unavailable if either historical side cannot be proved. Use a named **Backtest forecast** Task on the configured calculation. Pick up to eight completed weekly origins, from a source that can prove both what had taken effect and what had been recorded by each origin. Hold the method, scenario, scope and horizon constant, and pick origins before evaluating them. Re-run exactly the same calculation on facts available at that origin, including the purchase promises, bookings and open residuals known then. Apply today’s candidate assumptions only as a labelled scenario tested against past facts. A replay of a saved forecast instead uses that forecast’s recorded assumptions and predicted rows. Read later settled actuals through separate governed queries at an explicit evaluation cut. Score only comparable completed pairs. If historical coverage is missing, say “Backtest unavailable” for that origin and show the count omitted. A policy denial for required dependencies withholds the entire result using the generic response in §3. A late correction may affect actuals at the evaluation cut. It cannot retroactively enter the input. A business-date filter would let later corrections leak into the past. Scoring on the tuning window would overstate accuracy, and zero-filling could reward an incomplete source. [ADR-0018](ADR-0018-temporal-authority.md)

| Output | Compare against | Show by lead time and against a simple baseline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash close | Actual same-pool, same-currency book cash close at the same boundary | Mean absolute error and signed bias in currency. Baseline: carry the opening balance forward. |
| Demand and stock-out | Settled posted fulfilled sales in the same stock key and a complete observed physical on-hand close per date | Mean absolute error and bias in base units, with misses and false alarms for closing stock at or below zero. Baselines: same weekday last complete week for demand, unchanged opening on-hand for stock-out. |
| Rental utilisation | Settled actual-use intervals and actual rentable unit-hours for the same governed fleet group and periods | Mean absolute percentage-point error and signed bias. Baseline: confirmed-as-of bookings plus active possession without the scenario delay, using the same capacity basis. |

Signed bias is prediction minus actual. Show error per origin, valid pair counts by lead time, skipped origins and short display windows. Show an aggregate comparison for a metric/lead only with at least four comparable origins, and compare on the same paired sample for both methods. No skill or coverage claim is inferred from smaller samples. Each backtest uses the same permission rules as a forecast, one active slot, at most 40,000 admitted source facts across all origins and actuals, and one 60-second monotonic job deadline. Cap the full input and lineage at the same byte limits as standard runs, and count at most 1,000 score rows including summaries within the 2 MiB output cap. Reuse the run envelope and evidence stores with separately typed backtest rows, capture digests and scoring contract. A standard refresh captures anew. Rechecking recorded predictions against a new actuals cut produces a new backtest receipt. This gives a buyer a visible error in units and a controller an error in dollars. Percentage error would be undefined at zero cash. One score would hide lead-time differences. Scoring against another estimate would leave accuracy untested.

## Owner questions

1. **Should cash still show a projected balance if the bank balance and open obligations have different cut-offs?** Recommend withholding the projection until the cut reconciles. A single payment can otherwise appear as both cash on hand and expected income.
2. **Should scenario changes affect the company default immediately?** Recommend private what-if runs with a separate reviewed default revision. A controller can try moving payroll without changing the forecast that colleagues use.
3. **What should the first rental forecast promise?** Recommend committed utilisation of the company’s own serialized fleet. Label quote conversion, shared fleets and re-rent as unsupported until the sources can prove capacity and coverage.
4. **Should custom backtest scores appear beside verified standard scores?** Recommend displaying them inside Analyze as model-reported evidence. Standard scores can verify what was known at each origin independently of the calculation being evaluated.

# ADR-0088. Governed email, personal notifications and reminders

**Accepted, 2026-11-20,** after the 2026-11-20 Fable design read (ACCEPT WITH AMENDMENTS; twelve required amendments R1–R12 and nits N1–N5, all incorporated below, with the orchestrator rulings noted). The owner chartered COMMS-1 and requires a person's approval for every outward send.

An office worker can publish “two days before the agreed booking end, notify the owner and dispatch, and prepare the customer's return email.” The same declarations can remind a case owner about a review and prepare a quote or invoice from its record. The rentals inventory (§D, outgoing email and notification center) supplies those examples. The shared capability supplies the semantics once. SMS, staff chat and Outlook can later bind their own channel, consent and delivery contracts to the same thread, participant and business-object identities. ## One message from preparation through delivery

| Lane | Owns and publishes | Consumer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CONNECT-1 | Implements the Communication capability's **one canonical outbound message**, its revisions, pending-send reader and writers, including the proof-consuming approval transaction. It also owns the separate connection decision, scoped installation, outbox intent, provider worker and verified callback mapping. | The document action, Customize, workflows and reminders all call the same prepare operation. The identity origin reads this message by ID. |
| COMMS-1 | Defines the message terms and transitions below. Implements template and reminder revisions, recipient and consent policy, thread and inbox queries, and the reusable notification and preparation bindings. | Supplies governed inputs to CONNECT-1's writer and projects its receipts into the record and thread. |
| SIGNIN-1 slice 2b ([provider enablement and outward sends](../execution/packets/SIGNIN-1.md#slice-2b-provider-enablement-and-outward-sends), waiting for CONNECT-1's canonical pending records) | Issues the outward-send decision proof on the identity origin and joins proof consumption to CONNECT-1's writer. | A workflow approval, template approval or session login alone confers no send authority. |
| DOCS-1 | Owns document templates, clean publication, version handles, byte digests and current read/disclosure checks. | Communication owns the email subject and body template. An exact published PDF may be attached independently of how it was generated. |
| WORKFLOW-1 | Owns release-pinned runs, authority ceilings, journals, durable timers and recovery. | COMMS-1 binds its declared reminder and prepare/notify operations to that scheduler. N2-05 exercises the complete case on these contracts. |

A connector intent refers to the approved revision. The intent carries the delivery key and evidence references. The message remains the business record. CONNECT-1 can land the writer and decision reader before 2b with dispatch closed. SIGNIN-1 tests the production reader/writer join on that record before outward sends open. This splits implementation ownership without introducing a second pending-send table or a workflow-specific copy. ### What is reviewed

1. The released email action declares its source record type and governed query, ordered recipient selectors and roles, approved email template ID, purpose, document selectors and channel. A reminder or workflow selects that same action by stable ID. Its invocation names the action and saved record plus an expected revision or occurrence. Derive tenant, environment, acting company, record identity/revision, owner, customer and contact relationships from authenticated context and stored facts. Check a prepare grant and record scope. For a shared master require an authorized acting company. Resolve To/Cc only through declared, typed contact endpoints on that record or a governed relation. Refuse missing, ambiguous, hidden, suppressed, foreign-company or disallowed endpoints. The owner's COMMS ruling narrows the unshipped email action in ADR-0080 to stored sources and an approved template. ADR-0080 is amended in the same change (R5). Confirmation cannot add a typed address, substitute a subject/body or select an arbitrary file.
2. Store a stable thread linked to the business record and scoped participants. Each outbound occurrence has a stable message ID. Each immutable revision captures its source and query dependencies, purpose/channel, template ID/revision, exact rendered subject/body and digest, ordered recipient roles/contact IDs/endpoints, verified company sender and provider installation/account. For attachments store the ordered DOCS-1 version handles, digests and governed links to the record. Source links, scope and access are checked for each recipient's disclosure. Retain the issue and approval actors, release ID/root and activation fence. A new revision supersedes the previous review. Retry of the same preparation returns the existing revision under current authority.
3. The identity service loads ordered, labeled terms from CONNECT-1's narrow database reader. Show the rendered text, company, account/sender, purpose, every address and role, and each named attachment and exact version. Bind those terms, message/record/revision, versioned digest, attachment digests, release and fence, plus any run/occurrence, to the SIGNIN-1 outward-send challenge. Require a fresh user-verified passkey assertion for that action and outcome within five minutes. Only the isolated identity service issues the one-use proof bound to the session, person and scope.
4. Under lock the writer recomputes the same projection from stored facts, checks the proof, session and current confirm grant, and revalidates the source, consent, template, installation and exact document versions. In one transaction consume the proof, append the approval and audit, and create the trust-outbox row and one immutable send intent. Exact committed retries recover the receipt under current authority. A changed material term, grant or fence refuses. Switching release A → B → A still requires a new review. A workflow or reminder may prepare the message. A future unattended policy for a named class requires a separate owner ruling.

| Message status | Fact the user can inspect |
| --- | --- |
| drafted | A retained message revision exists. Its author can prepare a successor from governed sources. |
| awaiting approval | The exact revision is ready for a person's decision. A decline is audited and returns the message to drafted. |
| approved | The proof was consumed and its intent committed. Queued, retrying or held for reconciliation appears beside this status. |
| sent | The provider accepted the exact envelope. The receipt records its ID and acceptance time. Delivery is a distinct per-recipient fact. |
| failed | The send was definitively refused, or retries exhausted with proven non-acceptance. An uncertain acceptance stays held for reconciliation. |
| bounced | An authenticated bounce is correlated to an accepted recipient. Show the affected recipients and the outcomes of the others. Late acceptance or delivery cannot erase that bounce. |

Reconcile an in-flight or uncertain attempt before permitting a replacement occurrence. An intentional resend of a settled message creates a fresh occurrence and requires a fresh yes. Keep the original message, revisions, decision, per-recipient receipts, attempts and status history. Access to history and attachments follows current record, field and document permissions. Audit the exact send and any later outcome with scoped IDs/digests and timestamps. Keep message contents and addresses in governed storage.

## Delivery and customer preferences

Only the ADR-0080 worker sends. It derives an opaque provider key from the scoped installation, operation and occurrence, binds the exact envelope digest, and persists a fenced claim and attempt before I/O. Retries keep that key, envelope and account. At the last authorization check before handoff, recheck the release fence, approver's live rights, automation grants if applicable, verified sender, installation, consent, source-field disclosure and exact document access. Obtain the clean bytes from DOCS-1 under that scope. A stale approval or changed endpoint holds the whole envelope for a new decision. Never silently trim the reviewed recipients or substitute a newer attachment. [ADR-0078, record links and versions](ADR-0078-governed-documents-files-templates-and-signatures.md#record-links-versions-and-retention)

Use the connector's declared retryable outcomes, attempt and age caps, backoff, deadlines, quotas and provider deduplication window. After a crash following acceptance, reconcile using verified account, occurrence metadata and provider receipt. Retry only with proven non-acceptance or the original key inside its safe window. Otherwise retain the ambiguous attempt for an operator. Operator replay and restore use the same checks. An authenticated callback enters CONNECT-1's durable inbox once per scoped provider event ID. Check signature, freshness, account, stored message and recipient before mapping it. Quarantine a changed payload under the same ID or unknown correlation. Apply out-of-order events only when corroborated. [ADR-0080, delivery and inbound correlation](ADR-0080-connector-framework.md#from-a-committed-change-to-a-provider-receipt)

Each customer-facing recipient requires current permission for that company, channel, endpoint and declared purpose, with the basis defined by R6. Keep append-only consent and suppression events with provenance and effective time. Customer mail offers a provider-supported unsubscribe bound to its recipient and purpose. Consume its authenticated event through the same inbox, resolve its scope from stored correspondence, and record suppression idempotently. A hard bounce suppresses the endpoint across email purposes for that company, including another contact alias for the same address. A temporary bounce records its classification and follows the provider contract. A later delivery receipt cannot opt a recipient back in. Restore eligibility only through an authorized, evidenced consent or endpoint correction appropriate to the suppression. Serialize suppression with dispatch admission. A withdrawal committed before admission prevents the send, and one received after handoff governs future sends. Preserve the original sent record in both cases.

Real provider calls, including connection and health checks, require the explicitly enabled local demo until SIGNIN-1 proves its decision gates and CONNECT-1 admits pilot delivery. Every environment still requires a person's yes to send. CI uses network-free fakes with an acceptance ledger. Bind credentials only through ADR-0080's scoped installation slots resolved by the host at use time. The local demo uses its owner-readable file via `NORTH_STAR_CONNECTOR_KEY_FILE`. Patches, messages, logs, browser and assistant receive no credential values. [ADR-0080, company connections](ADR-0080-connector-framework.md#who-can-connect-a-company)

## What an owner can publish in Customize

Two versioned, closed patch members append a template revision or a reminder-rule revision. The email action is a third member, owned by CONNECT-1 (R11). Each includes its stable logical ID, new revision identity, expected predecessor (absent on creation), scope and an active or retired disposition. “Edit” appends content to the same ID. “Retire” appends a tombstone. Reject stale predecessors, reused revision IDs with different bytes and live references to a retired member. Permit coordinated replacement or retirement in an ordered change set. The active release selects one revision per ID. History retains the originally used revisions. The normal draft, plain diff, verify, approve and activate path applies to both human and assistant authors.

An email template binds its source query and record type, permitted purpose, bounded literal text and typed field placeholders in subject and plain-text body. The compiler checks that the action's source and purpose match the template and that each selected destination may receive the rendered content and visible recipient list. Bind fields by stable ID. Permit only approved query output and classifications for each output position. Reject undeclared paths, raw markup, expressions, header controls and unbounded values. Recheck every referenced field on one governed read snapshot for **the issuing person** at preview and issue. Refuse the render if any merged field is hidden or its source truncated or inaccessible. Preview produces neither a message nor an intent. For workflow preparation use the bounded automation principal and its live grant ceiling. Check the human reviewer's current read and disclosure rights before showing any text, and recheck the captured dependencies before dispatch. Template activation confers no access to the data it renders. [ADR-0078, governed template bindings](ADR-0078-governed-documents-files-templates-and-signatures.md#templates-and-pdf)

A reminder rule binds a stable record/occurrence query and date or instant anchor, eligibility predicate, positive bounded lead and unit, calendar source, recipient selectors (record owner or named company roles), and effects (inbox and/or prepare through a released email action). Record the actual role and owner resolutions at issue. A tenant can bind a governed event to the same internal notify operation in a release-authored workflow, selecting the record owner or a named role. The host checks the pinned operation grant and journals the occurrence. An inbox action can link to the saved record or its human task. The inbox is a scoped List per signed-in person. Deduplicate the owner who also holds the selected role. Check each person's active company membership and source-record/field grants before creating an entry, and recheck them for rows, counts, previews and links on every read. Only its recipient can mark an entry read or archive it. The internal path needs no outward approval and creates no provider intent.

## When a reminder fires

Use the workflow scheduler's durable timer, journal and verified active fence. For “N days before,” subtract N **civil dates** in the source's declared business calendar, retaining the local time or using the declared business-day boundary for a date-only anchor. Resolve each date independently in the pinned IANA zone. An elapsed-hour lead subtracts hours from the anchor instant. A working-day rule requires a governed, versioned list of working dates including exceptions for the whole horizon. Refuse that unit when this data is unavailable. Never assume Monday–Friday. Capture the anchor identity, source revision, calendar version/zone, boundary, lead and resolved UTC deadline. Apply ADR-0083 A1 to every derived boundary and refuse only a missing zone or working-day calendar (R8). [Tenant business day](ADR-0018-temporal-authority.md#the-tenant-owns-the-business-day) · [Workflow timers](ADR-0073-workflow-isolate-and-authority.md#timers-signals-and-external-delivery)

Identify one natural occurrence by tenant, environment, company, stable rule ID, record, and the source's stable business occurrence/anchor slot (e.g. this booking's agreed end). Keep revisions, timer IDs, deadlines, recipient membership and release fences out of this uniqueness key. A genuinely recurring source supplies a distinct stored cycle ID. Editing the date, lead or calendar reschedules a pending occurrence. Retirement, cancellation or loss of eligibility closes pending work. A fence change does not suspend an occurrence as a pinned run. R2 defines it as a release-derived registration that is re-derived under the same key. An already emitted occurrence remains consumed unless the source supplies a new anchor generation (R9).

At wake, lock the occurrence, compare the expected timer generation, re-read current eligibility and membership, and resolve recipients once. Atomically commit the occurrence, one inbox row per distinct eligible person and effect slot, and any pending email via CONNECT-1's prepare writer. Journal the result so replay returns its IDs. No network I/O occurs at wake. An unsatisfied declared email binding yields an inspectable refusal and no partial fan-out. Recover overdue work once while still eligible and before the anchor, otherwise record a missed occurrence. Bound catch-up batches, retain a durable cursor and rescan after restart. Use database instants for due work and an injected monotonic clock for elapsed worker budgets.

## Amendments from the 2026-11-20 Fable read

Each amendment carries the Fable wording of its fix, with the orchestrator's rulings where noted.

**R1. Prepare and notify are not confirmation-class.** ADR-0073's computed-inputs rule (N2-00) makes every Tier B operation confirmation-class. Prepare and notify take only stable IDs (action, record, expected revision or occurrence). They accept no body-computed content and write only an inert draft or an inbox row. They are therefore not confirmation-class. The send proof is the exact-values human decision. The prepare writer copies addresses and renders text itself from stored rows, so "never free text" holds at the database boundary rather than in COMMS-1 runtime code. ADR-0073 cross-references this ruling.

**R2. A reminder occurrence is a platform-owned registration, not a pinned guest run.** It is derived from the active release plus stored facts. Re-arming after a fence change is re-derivation under the same occurrence key. (a) It runs under a bounded automation principal whose authority ceiling is the rule's declared grants, never the author's or reviewer's. (b) Ruling: a release that carries reminder rules is workflow-bearing and a new external effect, so it is Manual under ADR-0085, consistent with the owner's workflow ruling. Its activation evidence counts the pending reminders and awaiting-approval messages it will invalidate. (c) The post-activation re-evaluation sweep is bounded and resumable. (d) Reminders run only where the workflow scheduler is enabled, which means the local demo until sign-in closes that item.

**R3. A hold voids the intent and the revision gets a new send occurrence.** A stale approval or changed endpoint before handoff voids the intent terminally under lock, because it is proven never handed off. The message returns to drafted. The successor revision's approval creates a new send occurrence with its own provider key. An intent with any attempt must reconcile first. The held and voided facts join the "beside approved" set in the status table. The provider key derives from the scoped installation, operation and send occurrence, not from the message ID alone.

**R4. Prepared messages expire and are tied to their occurrence.** A reminder- or workflow-prepared revision carries a not-after instant, defaulting to the anchor, in the reviewed terms. It is checked at approval and at dispatch. Closing the occurrence (retirement, cancellation or lost eligibility) withdraws its awaiting-approval message. After a fence change the R2 re-evaluation re-prepares it under the same occurrence without re-notifying the inbox. Ruling: batch send proofs (ADR-0081) are not admitted for sends in v1. It is one message per approval gesture.

**R5. ADR-0080 is amended in the same change.** The typed-address source is removed from the email action's closed union and the template reference is required. The slice 2 dependency sentence is rewritten to cite SIGNIN-1 slice 2b and the CONNECT-1 canonical message.

**R6. Purposes and the permission basis.** The purpose set is closed and versioned. Transactional purposes (quote, invoice, booking or rental notice, reminder about the customer's own transaction) are permitted on the basis of the stored business relationship, unless the recipient is suppressed. Any promotional purpose needs a recorded opt-in. A named authorized operation, owned by the consent writer in R11, records and corrects consent. The unsubscribe header and link are a deterministic function of the approved revision and the recipient, and the envelope digest covers them, so nothing reviewed changes after approval. Normalize addresses before suppression matching.

**R7. Retention.** The message, its revisions, decision, receipts and attempts take a minimum retention class with no ordinary purge operation. A message's attachment handle is an ADR-0078 active reference that blocks purge of that document version. Inbox entries state their own, shorter retention and an archive rule. Inbound replies are out of scope for v1.

**R8. Derived deadlines follow ADR-0083 A1 and ADR-0087.** A gap resolves to the first valid instant after it and a repeated boundary to the earlier offset. For example a booking ending 2026-03-09 02:30 in Edmonton, one civil day before, is 2026-03-08 03:00 MDT. Pin the boundary-rule and tzdb versions with the occurrence. Refuse only for a missing zone or working-day calendar. Take the zone and day boundary from the anchor field's ADR-0087 capture, including perRecordIana. The rule's calendar supplies only working-day lists. For a date-only inclusive interval, "end" means the last entered date. "Reminder anchor" is added to ADR-0087's consumer admission table. Merged date-times render in the record zone with offset under the no-viewer rule. N2: capture the tzdb version with the armed deadline. A later tzdb change keeps the pinned UTC deadline.

**R9. Moved anchors re-remind in v1.** Ruling: SUPPORT. This is the headline rental case, a booking extended after its end reminder fired. The occurrence key includes a stored anchor generation supplied by the source. For Booking that is the agreed-end revision from `booking.changed/v1`. An extension creates a new occurrence. An unchanged anchor never re-fires. A genuinely recurring source still supplies its own cycle ID.

**R10. The inbox is bounded.** State a maximum of resolved recipients per occurrence and refuse, not truncate, above it. Page by keyset with a fixed page size. Cap the unread count ("99+") so the per-read grant recheck is bounded. An entry's text is a fixed template plus a record link, with the same hidden-field refusal as email.

**R11. Patch members, channel and writers.** Three versioned, closed members exist: `saveEmailTemplate` (schemaVersion 1), `saveReminderRule` (schemaVersion 1) and the extended `addDocumentEmailAction` (schemaVersion 2, adding source query, purpose, channel and the required template). CONNECT-1 owns the email action, since its writer consumes it, and it is CONNECT-1 slice 2's first deliverable with its own rung and replay test. Channel is a versioned discriminated union on the action, template and reminder effect, initially `{ kind: "email" }`. The prior parser refuses an unknown kind. Each kind's real conflict maps to ADR-0085's closed codes (RETIRED_TARGET, INCOMPATIBLE_TYPE, INCOMPATIBLE_SCOPE). Single writer lane per row: CONNECT-1 writes thread and participant rows. COMMS-1 writes consent events and suppression rows through published functions. Lock order, followed by every writer, worker and callback handler: reminder occurrence, thread, participant, message, consent, suppression, send intent. Dispatch admission takes the suppression lock before the intent, so a withdrawal committed before admission prevents the send.

**R12. Slice plan.** Slice 1 adds the typed email-endpoint field on contact records and the consent and suppression store. Slice 2 is jointly gated on SIGNIN-1 slice 2b and DOCS-1 version handles. Slices 3 and 4 are local-demo only while the scheduler is off. Slice 4 acceptance adds the gap case (R8) and the moved-anchor case (R9). See [COMMS-1](../execution/packets/COMMS-1.md).

**Nits.** N3: a message shows bounced when any recipient bounced, with the per-recipient facts authoritative. N4: the person who prepared a message may also approve it. The owner ruling requires no maker-checker. N5: an approval refused because the approver cannot read a merged field refuses entirely and never shows a redacted message.
